# Sources and evidence limits

## Main case

Oxilia et al. (2015), **Earliest evidence of dental caries manipulation in the Late Upper Palaeolithic**, Scientific Reports 5, 12150. [Article](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep12150) · [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26179739/) · [Open-access manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4504065/). DOI: 10.1038/srep12150.

The study examined the lower right third molar of the Villabruna individual from northern Italy, dated approximately 14,160–13,820 calibrated years before present. The authors interpreted microscopic striations, enamel chipping and experimental replication as evidence of ante-mortem cavity manipulation with pointed flint during scratching/levering. The Short rounds the date to about 14,000 years ago and attributes the inference to researchers and experiments.

The paper title contains a historical priority claim from 2015. **This Short does not call Villabruna the first dentist, earliest dentistry or oldest treated tooth.** More recent research includes [a 2026 PLOS ONE report on Neanderthal dental intervention](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0347662). That separate case is not blended into the Villabruna story.

## What is reconstructed

- Kai holding his cheek, Ena's support, camp, clothes, exact feelings and conversations are fictional visual storytelling. No claim identifies these characters as the excavated person or the actual operator.
- The tooth, chipped edge, microscopic scratches, flint, map and research scenes are **new schematic illustrations**, not photographs, scans, traced measurements or the original specimen. Evidence scenes carry an on-screen Illustration label. Relative sizes are conceptual, not to scale.
- The study does not establish the subjective pain, identity of a helper, whether treatment relieved pain, or a universal prehistoric treatment practice. The narration states these limits explicitly.
- A contemporary clinic in the final joke is a separate modern setting, not a claim that prehistoric people had modern dentistry.
- No dental procedure is demonstrated on a living mouth. This is historical storytelling, not treatment guidance.
