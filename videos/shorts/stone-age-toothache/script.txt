Your tooth hurts. It is fourteen thousand years ago. There is no dental clinic down the road. So did people simply do nothing?

Not always. At Villabruna, in northern Italy, researchers studied a human molar with a cavity. Inside it, they found tiny scratches and chipped enamel.

Experiments suggested that pointed flint tools had scraped the cavity while the person was alive. Think careful scraping, not a modern dentist's drill.

That is evidence of an attempt to treat a damaged tooth. It does not tell us exactly how it felt, who helped, or whether the treatment worked.

The person holding their cheek here is imaginary. The ancient tooth is real. Our ancestors had serious skills. I would still rather book an appointment.
