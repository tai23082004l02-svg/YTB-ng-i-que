Your tooth hurts. It is 14,000 years ago. What happens next?

A stick-figure history Short about a remarkable archaeological clue: the Villabruna molar from northern Italy. Researchers interpreted marks inside its cavity as evidence of manipulation with pointed flint tools while the individual was alive.

The character is imaginary. The archaeological case is real. We do not know exactly how it felt, who helped, or whether it relieved pain. This is one documented case, not a universal prehistoric routine or a claim about the earliest dentistry.
