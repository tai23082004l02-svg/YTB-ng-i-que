Could modern humanity survive the forces behind Earth's five great mass extinctions? Our technology gives us powerful advantages—but food, water, electricity, and cooperation still have to work together.

This illustrated scientific thought experiment revisits the Late Ordovician, Late Devonian, end-Permian, end-Triassic, and end-Cretaceous crises. For each one, we separate ancient evidence from a hypothetical modern scenario and ask what our tools could protect, how long they could last, and what might fail first.

We explore changing climates, oxygen loss in the oceans, volcanic warming and cooling, asteroid defense, impact winter, resilient food research, and what scientists mean by a possible sixth mass extinction.

Individual survival, the persistence of human populations, and the survival of today's civilization are different questions. This video gives conditional assessments, not invented survival percentages or a prediction that a particular disaster is about to happen.
