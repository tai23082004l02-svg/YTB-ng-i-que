# Sources and interpretation notes — Before Us

This original illustrated documentary investigates the **detectability** of a hypothetical industrial civilization in deep geological time. It does not announce an ancient technological discovery, depict a verified prehuman city, or assign a numerical probability to an earlier civilization.

## Core paper

Gavin A. Schmidt and Adam Frank, **The Silurian Hypothesis: Would it be possible to detect an industrial civilization in the geological record?** Open preprint posted in 2018; accepted for publication in the International Journal of Astrobiology. The accessible preprint's scope, preservation discussion, proposed geochemical tracers, comparison events and discussion were consulted directly.

- Open abstract and PDF: https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03748
- Journal DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/S1473550418000095

The authors explicitly express strong doubt that a previous industrial civilization existed before ours. Their question concerns how evidence might survive and how industry could be distinguished from natural processes. The film preserves that distinction.

The paper's word “industrial” has a specific scope concerning large-scale energy use and environmental effects. Its principal deep-time analysis concerns pre-Quaternary industrial activity after complex life on land, not an exhaustive test of every possible society in all of Earth history. The hypothesis does not establish a lost civilization in the Silurian Period, and its name is a reference to fiction rather than a paleontological discovery. The film uses no copyrighted Doctor Who character images or actual author portraits.

## What the core paper supports

- Direct objects and recognizable individuals may be difficult to discover across deep time because of preservation, limited original footprint and limited later sampling. Difficulty does not demonstrate an earlier civilization.
- Stable isotope changes, sediment delivery, mineralogy, metal distributions, synthetic materials and biological changes can be considered as components of a potential industrial fingerprint.
- Many individual signals can have natural causes. A coordinated pattern may be informative but still needs context and alternative explanations.
- The ultimate very-long-term fate of plastics and many synthetic compounds is uncertain. Present-day environmental distribution does not guarantee that a recognizable bottle, circuit board or polymer will survive for fifty million years.
- Radioactive isotopes are not interchangeable clocks. Many modern markers have unsuitable half-lives for deep-time questions. Longer-lived proposed tracers require sufficient production, preservation, detectability and evaluation of natural sources; the video gives no isotope-production instructions.
- The paper discusses natural fission in the Franceville Basin/Oklo context as a reminder that a technological-sounding phenomenon need not have an industrial origin.
- The authors discuss both natural false positives and the possibility that assumptions about a civilization's behavior influence the footprint expected. Lower detectability is not positive evidence of a clean, invisible civilization.

## Observed modern industrial footprint

Waters et al. (2016), **The Anthropocene is functionally and stratigraphically distinct from the Holocene**, Science, DOI 10.1126/science.aad2622. Abstract consulted through Europe PMC's bibliographic record. The review describes manufactured materials, combustion particles, fallout markers, altered element cycles and biological changes as components of the observed modern stratigraphic footprint. Observing these markers now does not guarantee that every marker remains detectable after fifty million years. Its naming discussion is historical, not a current ratification announcement.
https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aad2622

## PETM comparison

Zeebe, Ridgwell and Zachos (2016), **Anthropogenic carbon release rate unprecedented during the past 66 million years**, Nature Geoscience. Abstract consulted for the difficulty of reconstructing release rates from geological records and the difference between resemblance in environmental signatures and equivalence in timing or cause.

https://www.nature.com/articles/ngeo2681

The PETM occurred approximately 56 million years ago. The film does not identify it as one of the Big Five or as an industrial event. The original Silurian paper compares aspects of its geological signatures with an expected industrial fingerprint while discussing natural carbon-release mechanisms. No precise current warming total or 2026 emissions estimate is copied from older papers. Values described as current in a 2018 paper are not treated as current news in this documentary.

## Preservation and the geological archive

The film's city-abandonment sequence is a thought experiment, not a dated forecast. Different environments can expose, bury, preserve, erode, alter or remove material differently. No universal lifetime is assigned to every building, metal, foundation or city. Tectonic recycling does not mean every continental surface is destroyed on the same schedule, and the video gives no absolute maximum age for all seafloor.

Layer thickness depends on deposition, duration, mixing, compaction, erosion and missing intervals. The paper's discussion of thin industrial intervals is not presented as proof that all human civilization will form an identical millimeter-thick layer everywhere.

The sampling, contamination, intrusive-material and independent-replication scenes explain general principles of evidential context. They are not a reconstruction of an actual unusual sample or an actual discovery. A younger particle in old rock does not automatically share the age of its host layer.

## Scientific naming and scope

“Anthropocene” is used to discuss human influence and a possible geological fingerprint, without claiming that formal geological naming decides whether the underlying observations exist. The film does not repeat the 2018 preprint's then-pending naming discussion as a current status announcement.

The fictional future investigator is a storytelling avatar, not an evolutionary prediction that today's human appearance or societies will persist for fifty million years. The fifty-million-year framing is an explanatory timescale, not an assigned age for a purported artifact.

The off-world-archive discussion is a qualified extension of the detection problem: other environments have different preservation processes, not guaranteed perfect preservation. No lunar base, unexplained image, spacecraft or remote atmospheric signal is presented as evidence of an earlier technological civilization.

## What would count as stronger evidence?

The documentary calls for reliable context, reproducible observations, independent measurements, meaningful comparisons across sites and explicit tests of natural alternatives. These are methodological criteria, not a claim that any existing dataset has satisfied them for a prehuman industrial civilization. Nor is a missing record used to assign equal probabilities to arbitrary stories.

## Illustrations and presentation

All new artwork is AI-generated 2D illustration. Rocks, cores, samples, instruments, diagrams, cities and hypothetical future scenes are conceptual or artistic representations, not source photographs or raw research data. Any imagined skyline is visually separated as a thought or comparison rather than presented as a verified excavated structure. No intact manufactured object beside an ancient fossil is offered as proof.

The voice, subtitles, thumbnail and metadata are newly produced. The open paper was consulted as a source; no downloaded full paper, institutional photograph or copyrighted footage is republished in the video assets.
