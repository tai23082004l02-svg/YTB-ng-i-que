# Sources and evidence notes

This is an original popular-science script, not a systematic review. Fossil ranges are approximate and refer to known evidence, not exact dates of speciation or the death of a final individual. Accessed during production on 24 October 2026.

## Core references

1. Smithsonian Human Origins, **Homo sapiens**. African origins and approximate 300,000-year history.
   https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-sapiens
2. Smithsonian Human Origins, **Homo neanderthalensis**. Anatomy, broad distribution, approximate time range, tools and subsistence. Individual claims about symbolism, burial and care remain debated; the script avoids flower-burial claims and does not infer a complete religion.
   https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-neanderthalensis
3. Smithsonian Human Origins, **Homo floresiensis**. Fossils approximately 100,000–60,000 years old; associated tool record extends to approximately 50,000 years ago. These are different evidence ranges. Small adult body size is not childhood.
   https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-floresiensis
4. Smithsonian Human Origins, **Homo erectus**. Very long temporal range; youngest widely accepted fossils substantially predate a 50,000-year-old illustrative encounter.
   https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-erectus
5. Smithsonian Human Origins, **Homo naledi**. Known fossils approximately 335,000–236,000 years old. Temporal overlap does not establish direct contact with sapiens.
   https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/human-fossils/species/homo-naledi
6. Détroit et al. (2019), **A new species of Homo from the Late Pleistocene of the Philippines**, Nature. Abstract consulted for the limited Callao Cave remains and the morphological basis for Homo luzonensis. Whole-body appearance is not reconstructed as established fact.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1067-9
7. Hublin et al. (2017), **New fossils from Jebel Irhoud, Morocco and the pan-African origin of Homo sapiens**, Nature. Abstract consulted: mosaic morphology and approximately 315 ± 34 thousand-year age, rounded in narration.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/nature22336
8. Slon et al. (2018), **The genome of the offspring of a Neanderthal mother and a Denisovan father**, Nature. Abstract consulted: Denisova 11 and direct first-generation parentage. The script does not invent the family's language, circumstances or personal history.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-018-0455-x
9. Skov et al. (2022), **Genetic insights into the social organization of Neanderthals**, Nature. Open-access abstract and main text consulted: 13 sampled individuals, close relatives, and evidence concerning one small Siberian community. This is not treated as a census of all Neanderthals.
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-022-05283-y
10. Huerta-Sánchez et al. (2014), **Altitude adaptation in Tibetans caused by introgression of Denisovan-like DNA**, Nature. Abstract consulted: the EPAS1-region haplotype, Denisovan-related introgression and high-altitude adaptation. The script does not present one gene as a full explanation of Tibetan physiology.
    https://www.nature.com/articles/nature13408
11. Sankararaman et al. (2014), **The genomic landscape of Neanderthal ancestry in present-day humans**, Nature. Abstract consulted for introgression, inherited variants and selection. Ancestry estimates vary by population and method.
    https://www.nature.com/articles/nature12961
12. Smithsonian Human Origins, **Ancient DNA and Neanderthals** and **Social Life**. Background on methods, ancestry and social cooperation.
    https://humanorigins.si.edu/evidence/genetics/ancient-dna-and-neanderthals
    https://humanorigins.si.edu/human-characteristics/social-life

## What the video does and does not claim

- There is no known total count of prehistoric tribes. Tribes, archaeological cultures, populations, species and genetic lineages are different categories.
- The cast is illustrative rather than a complete inventory. Denisovan lineage identification does not depend on a universally agreed binomial species name.
- Different relatives did not all live at the same time, inhabit the same place, meet one another or disappear for one demonstrated reason.
- Climate, demography, competition, social networks and assimilation are explanatory candidates with region-dependent evidence. A global extermination campaign, a universal intelligence ranking and a single proven extinction mechanism are not asserted.
- All living human populations are Homo sapiens. Archaic ancestry percentages are neither hierarchies nor species labels for people alive today.
- Camp scenes, personal appearances, clothing, expressions and family vignettes are artistic reconstructions. Denisovan faces are especially speculative. Illustrations of DNA, branch connections and laboratory procedures are conceptual, not raw scientific data.
- The direct evidence for Denisova 11 is parentage inferred from DNA. An illustration of parents and a child is a visual explanation, not a recovered portrait.
- Ancient communities' individual emotions and private memories cannot be recovered from a fossil. Narrative examples are identified as possibilities rather than documented biographies.
- The references support factual background. The script's bridges, camp scenes, street encounter and bridge analogy are original explanatory devices.
