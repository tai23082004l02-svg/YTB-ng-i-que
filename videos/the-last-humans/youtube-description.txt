TITLE
Earth Once Had Many Human Species. Why Are We the Only Ones Left?

DESCRIPTION
For much of our history, being human did not mean being Homo sapiens. Neanderthals, Denisovans, the small-bodied people of Flores, and other relatives shared parts of a much more complicated world.

Why did their distinct branches disappear, while ours survived? This original illustrated documentary follows the evidence from fossil bones and stone tools to ancient DNA. We investigate climate, small populations, competition, cooperation, and interbreeding—without pretending that one explanation solves every disappearance.

We also separate species from tribes, show why overlapping dates do not prove encounters, and ask what it means to be the only surviving human species when some relatives still live on in our ancestry.

All living human populations are Homo sapiens. Archaic ancestry is population history, not a hierarchy.
