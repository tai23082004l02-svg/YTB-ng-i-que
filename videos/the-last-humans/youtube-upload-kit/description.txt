Why is Homo sapiens the only human species alive today? The answer lies in fossil bones, ancient DNA, and a surprisingly tangled family tree.

Meet the Neanderthals, Denisovans, the small-bodied people of Flores, and other ancient relatives. Then follow the evidence behind their disappearance: changing climates, small populations, competition, social connections, and interbreeding.

Along the way, we explore the child with a Neanderthal mother and a Denisovan father, the limits of the fossil record, and the ancient ancestry that still survives in people today.

These groups did not all live at the same time or in the same place. Researchers are still investigating why particular lineages disappeared. All living human populations belong to Homo sapiens; ancestry percentages are not a hierarchy.
