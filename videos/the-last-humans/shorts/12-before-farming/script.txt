Farming did not make us the last surviving human species.
Neanderthals disappeared around forty thousand years ago. Agriculture began spreading much later, with major developments roughly twelve thousand years ago in some regions.
Those dates are approximate, and farming did not begin everywhere at once. The important point is the gap: tens of thousands of years.
Cities, writing, and modern industry came later still.
Our ancestors were already the only surviving human species before they built the things we often use to explain human success.
Their survival has to be investigated through earlier environments, population history, social connections, expansion, and interbreeding.
None of those factors supplies one proven answer for every place.
We tell this story from the surviving branch. That can make its outcome feel inevitable, even when people living through it had no way to know the ending.
