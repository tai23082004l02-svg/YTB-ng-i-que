Fifty thousand years ago, meeting another human did not always mean meeting someone from your species.
Homo sapiens shared parts of the prehistoric world with other human lineages. Neanderthals lived across parts of Europe and Asia. Denisovan-related groups had their own complicated Asian history. Small-bodied humans also lived on Flores.
But this was not one giant reunion. Different groups occupied different places, and their known dates do not put everyone beside the same campfire.
Today, every living human belongs to Homo sapiens. That does not make the past a tournament where every other branch was inferior.
Some relatives disappeared as distinct populations. Some also contributed ancestry to later people through interbreeding.
We are the only human species alive today. Our family history still contains more than one kind of human.
