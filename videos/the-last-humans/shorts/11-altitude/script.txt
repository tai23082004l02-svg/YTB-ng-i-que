A meeting between ancient human lineages helped leave an inheritance useful at high altitude.
Research on a DNA region near the gene called E P A S one found evidence of a Denisovan-related version in Tibetan ancestry.
That inherited region was favored in an environment where oxygen availability is low.
It is a striking example of interbreeding contributing to later adaptation.
But one gene does not explain every aspect of Tibetan physiology. Environment, other genes, and population history matter too. Nor does this result give us a complete biography of the ancient group that contributed the DNA.
Genetic evidence can preserve traces of a meeting across human lineages, long after the people themselves are gone.
An extinct branch can leave more than a fossil. Sometimes it leaves an inheritance that remains useful in a very different future.
